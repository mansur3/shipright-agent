# Step 4: Findings ledger

Every number is produced by a script on the cleaned data (rules in 04_data_quality.md). Reproduce with `python -I <script>` from `verification/`; raw output is saved as `out_<script>.txt`.
Scripts: `h_claims.py` (H01-H10, also writes `claim_flags.csv`, one row per claim with its flags), `h_sales.py` (H11-H17), `h_stock.py` (H18-H26), `h_fefo.py` (H27-H35, scheme-driven claims run rate), `h_lost.py` (lost-sales estimate), `h_outw_check.py` (what `avg_monthly_outward_packs` is).
Rupee kinds are never added together: **C** cash already credited, **S** stock at risk, **F** future leakage preventable, **R** revenue at stake (at PTS, before margin; margin is not in the pack).
Period: Apr 2024 to Sep 2026 (30 months, FY25 Q1 to FY27 Q2). "Last 4 quarters" = FY26 Q3 to FY27 Q2 (Oct 2025 to Sep 2026) unless said otherwise. 1 crore = 100 lakh.

## 3. Findings the pack did not anticipate
- **F-A. Claims are overwhelmingly valid on the data we can check.** 4,112 credited claims; clear policy breaches amount to INR 51.5 lakh over 30 months (see section 4). Most of the INR 79.2 lakh "duplicate" and INR 82.7 lakh "zero match" in the existing ShipRight deck is either partial claims within supply (INR 70 lakh) or claims on archived batches (INR 85 lakh).
- **F-B. The QBR fill rate counts free scheme packs as supplied.** It ties only on (invoiced + free) / ordered. Counting only what was ordered, FY27 Q1 is 88.0% not 90.9%, and FY27 Q2 is 82.6% (84.8% on the QBR basis). The gap grows with the free-goods percentage, so the scheme hides part of the shortage.
- **F-C. Orders are rising while supply is flat.** Ordered value per quarter rose from INR 46.8 crore (FY25 Q1) to INR 64.0 crore (FY27 Q2); invoiced value has stayed at INR 49 to 53 crore a quarter since FY26 Q1. Whether this is lost demand or order padding by stockists who expect cuts cannot be told from the pack (Q22).
- **F-D. Short and long at once is an allocation split problem.** At the same month-end Lucknow has no stock on 20 SKUs while the rest of the network holds enough to cover its demand for 20 months; Kolkata's cover is 3.8 months on 15% of outward.
- **F-E. Hooghly FEFO is the only operational control that broke, and it broke at one site.** 0% breaches at five CFAs in every quarter, 54% to 64% at Kolkata in the last two quarters.
- **F-F. The CFO's 3% gap is mostly a scheme-design effect.** Expiry claims logged: INR 0.32 crore (FY25 Q2) to 1.68 crore (FY27 Q2); scheme packs account for most of the excess (H13).

## 5. The CFO's forecasting ask (evidence for Step 5)
- Short and long at the same time is real (F-D), but it is an allocation split, not a forecast: Lucknow is out of 20 SKUs while the network holds enough stock for 20 months of its demand.
- A forecast that knows the quarter rhythm already reaches about 10% MAPE (H23), better than Demand Planning's 14% before any AI; the vendor's "30% improvement" from 14% is about 10%.
- Expiry returns are mostly scheme-pushed packs (H13); a more accurate forecast of a demand pattern created by a scheme does not stop those packs being claimed.
- The fill rate falls at all CFAs now, and is flattered by free packs in the QBR (F-B).
- A forecast is still useful as an input, but it would not change items 1, 2, 3 or 4 above by itself.

### Added in Step 5: how much shortage a forecast could address (h_forecast_share.py, out_h_forecast_share.txt)
Shortfall = packs needed to lift each SKU x CFA cell to one month of its own outward, valued at PTS, at six month-ends Apr to Sep 2026: INR 10.18 crore in total (0.71+0.33, 0.83, 1.42+0.99, 1.60+0.12, 1.28, 1.61+1.27 by month-end, allocation-fixable plus remainder). 73% could be met from stock above one month of cover at other CFAs (allocation); 27% (INR 2.7 crore) could not be (supply or forecast).
